# Complete bounded enumeration of two-small-step AM chains

**Status: finite verification.** The checks establish only the stated finite range.


**Status. Written completeness argument and exhaustive finite enumeration.** Start with 1. Values strictly increase. At each write sum or multiply any two earlier values, including the same operand twice. Count value sequences, retaining one legal recipe if multiple operations yield the same next value. Set λ(1)=0 and λ(n)=bit_length(bit_length(n)−1) for n≥2, equivalent to the published floor of the double logarithm plus one. For m≥2 the next value cannot exceed m², so the level can rise by at most one. The first write is 2 and raises the level once. Consequently the length of a chain ending at n is λ(n) plus its number of level-preserving steps.

Depth-first enumeration emits every legal next value up to L. If the small-step budget is spent, the next value must reach the next threshold 2^(2^λ(m)). Induction on prefixes gives soundness, completeness and uniqueness of each value sequence. For L=10^18, λ≤6, hence at most eight writes for two small steps. A chain with fewer writes to the same endpoint must have fewer small steps, and monotonicity prevents exceeding its endpoint on the way. Subtracting the zero- and one-step endpoint sets therefore identifies the integers whose *shortest* chain has exactly two small steps. These counts do not classify the infinite family asked for in the paper. [Tarek, Bahig and Anwar](https://doi.org/10.3934/Math.2026667)

**An unbounded subclass.** If a chain has two consecutive small steps and all subsequent steps are squarings, take the prefix before the pair. With zero small steps it is the tower 1,2,4,16,…,A: at each threshold only the maximum possible value reaches the next threshold (at 2 doubling ties squaring). Let P be that prefix. Choose x from the sums and products of pairs in P with A<x<A². Choose y from pairs in P∪{x} with x<y<A². Append any number of squarings of y. This parameterization is necessary and sufficient for the stated value-sequence family: the comparisons keep both new values in A's level, and squaring increases the level exactly once. It classifies witnesses in this subclass; determining minimum defect allows other chains to the same endpoint.
