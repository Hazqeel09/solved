# Exact synthesis by target saturation and helper branching

**Status: research proof draft; independent mathematical review pending.** Automated evidence is scoped below and does not certify the complete argument.

Cantaloube and coauthors organize MCM search around non-target intermediates and propose generalizing their propagator to straight-line programs. The following gives a complete construction for bounded positive integer arithmetic with fixed-cost +, −, × and exact division, arbitrary finite seeds and multiple required targets. Storage is unrestricted. This is an adaptation of the target-saturation principle to our arithmetic model. [2026 paper, Property 4.5 and conclusion](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol379-cp2026/html/LIPIcs.CP.2026.11/LIPIcs.CP.2026.11.html)

Fix seed set S, target set T, magnitude cap C and helper budget h. A helper is a distinct computed value outside S∪T. Let q=|S∪T|+h and w be the bit length of C.

**Theorem.** A target-covering arithmetic program using at most h helpers exists if and only if the following search succeeds. Before every helper decision, repeatedly compute every currently obtainable missing target until this closure stops changing. Then branch only on a new non-target result of one legal operation on two available operands. Stop once every target is available. Search helper layers in increasing order. At most [2q(q+1)]ʰ times a polynomial in q and w arithmetic work is sufficient. The procedure is polynomial for each fixed h. This bound is exponential in h and depends on the number of targets; it does not establish fixed-parameter tractability in h alone.

**Proof.** In unlimited storage any repeated write can use its earliest occurrence. Every target outside S is written once and every helper adds one write. The optimum has cost |T∖S| plus the minimum number of helpers. Computing an already reachable target early cannot remove a value or invalidate an operation. Its write is owed in every completed program. Thus all target writes may be saturated before the next helper without increasing final cost. Any given feasible program has a first remaining helper whose operands are seeds, earlier helpers or earlier targets. The saturation closure contains all those earlier targets by induction on their original order. Branching on this helper therefore preserves a route to the given solution. Repeating proves completeness. Every emitted operation is checked for positivity, cap and divisibility, which proves soundness.

There are at most q available values. Each unordered operand pair offers at most four distinct positive results for these operations. Thus at most 2q(q+1) helper branches are possible at each of h levels. Saturation performs at most |T| rounds of finite pair enumeration. The procedure branches on generated values, with no enumeration of every integer up to C. Histories reaching the same helper set have the same target closure and may be merged. Their retained witness supplies a construction. The same argument applies to any fixed finite collection of deterministic bounded-arity operations whenever feasibility is testable and values can be retained freely.

## Verification and scope

The implementation uses exact positive-integer arithmetic with explicit cap and divisibility checks. Against a separate search over every instruction and every retained value set, all 180 randomized small instances agreed for helper budgets zero through two, including multi-target dependencies, subtraction, exact division, duplicate seeds and infeasible cases. Every emitted witness was replayed. Starting from 1, targets 15 and 17 require exactly three helpers and five writes. Targets 5 and 6 from seeds 2 and 3 require zero helpers and two writes in this model. [Implementation](../../solved/helper_search.py) · [Exhaustive-state comparison](../../tests/test_helper_search.py)

The latter example also exposes the storage condition. With only two registers, producing 5 overwrites a needed seed and producing 6 first does the same. Two instructions cannot visit both targets. Three suffice by writing 5, restoring 2=5−3 and multiplying 2×3. Thus target saturation preserves the optimum only under its retention hypothesis. A fixed-register execution requires a separate scheduling argument.
