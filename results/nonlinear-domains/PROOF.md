# Divisor certificates for nonlinear fractional-linear domains

**Status: research proof draft; independent mathematical review pending.** Automated evidence is scoped below and does not certify the complete argument.

**Claim.** Fix C1's connected template and its root x. For a balanced component, take every transported vertex value and every executed prefix. The affine guards give a progression P as in C3. For each nonlinear guard (ax+b)/(cx+d), write (c,d)=g(p,q) with p>0 and gcd(p,q)=1. Every admissible root satisfies 0≠px+q dividing pb−qa. For guards with the same (p,q), their denominator divides the gcd G of the absolute values of these nonzero integers. Each such pole therefore covers all feasible roots with at most 2τ(G) candidates, independent of the machine cap. This is an upper bound; a root must still satisfy all prefix guards.

**Proof.** An integral result y satisfies g(px+q)y=ax+b. Multiply by p and subtract a(px+q) to obtain (pg y−a)(px+q)=pb−qa. Invertibility makes the right side nonzero. List the signed divisors d of G for any one pole, form (d−q)/p when integral, intersect with P, and replay every guard. Balance gives all terminal identities, positivity excludes zero divisors, and the retained intermediate guards enforce exact division. The converse follows by executing the paths. The same-pole gcd is only a necessary filter; for example 2x/4 before cancellation still requires x even.

The prime support of G is inherited. Primitive matrices for addition or subtraction have unit determinant. Multiplications, divisions and inverse terminal maps introduce determinant factors only from their positive operands or target. Composition multiplies determinants; taking a projective inverse preserves determinant up to a rational square arising from normalization. In the actual integer implementation the adjugate has the original determinant, and primitive normalization divides the integer determinant by a square. Dividing by g and taking gcds can only remove prime factors. Thus one can factor the small primitive operands by gcd with the remaining G instead of factoring a potentially enormous product from a long path. The implementation enumerates divisors of gcd factors through 10^18; larger inherited factors outside its supported range require a separate factorization procedure. Enumeration has output-dependent cost; this is not a polynomial-time bound in the input bit length.

If the progression already has fewer candidates, scanning it is sufficient. If neither scan fits the explicit budget, the transmitted progression plus the complete list of guarded fractional-linear maps still describes the exact domain. A budget cutoff is never an emptiness certificate. Intersect every component guard and combine coincident poles before expanding candidates. This extends the 2026 CP work's idea of branching on non-target choices to this restricted family; its hardware operation model is different.
