# Extremal values and optimal simultaneous cancellation

**Status: research proof draft; independent mathematical review pending.** Automated evidence is scoped below and does not certify the complete argument.

The machine starts with a nonempty finite set of positive integers and can use addition, positive subtraction, multiplication and exact positive division. Values can be reused. First consider unlimited storage and no upper cap. Write M and S for the largest and second largest distinct values that can have appeared in at most k instructions, including initial values.

**Arbitrary initial bank.** Let H≥2 be the largest seed and J the second largest distinct seed when present. For k≥1, M=H^(2^k). For H≥3 put B=max(2H,HJ), omitting HJ if J is absent. For H=2 put B=3 if 1 is available and B=2 otherwise. At k=1 the runner-up is B. For k≥2 it is

    S = max(B^(2^(k−1)), (H³)^(2^(k−2))).

To attain M every step must attain the maximum possible new peak, namely its square. Doubling ties squaring when the peak is 2, producing exactly the same numerical history. At the first step a different peak is at most B and B is reachable, including by retaining an earlier value. At a later first deviation, just before the write the maximum m is at least 4 and its runner-up is √m. A different peak is at most m√m: the largest nonsquare product has that value, the largest addition is 2m, and positive division and subtraction cannot increase the peak. The bound is attained by multiplying the last two spine values and then squaring. The resulting final runner-up bound is independent of this later deviation's position. Histories shorter than k and earlier outputs on the maximum history are covered as well. With only seed 1, the first increase must be to 2. For k≥2 one obtains Mₖ=2^(2^(k−1)) and Sₖ=3^(2^(k−2)). At zero writes return the two largest distinct seeds if present.

These upper bounds still apply after imposing a finite cap and register limit. They are sharp on the constrained instance whenever the indicated constructions fit. They do not require division to have a fixed cost in a different computational model. The published subtraction comparison already uses tower-minus-one witnesses and extremal AMC estimates. The arbitrary-bank formula, shared-target certificate, and application to the signed real defect are the deductions made here. [Saranurak and Jindal, esp. Lemmas 22–23 and Theorem 24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.2549) · [The 2026 one-small-step AM classification](https://doi.org/10.3934/Math.2026667).

**An optimal multi-target family.** Suppose m different targets lie strictly between Sₖ and Mₖ. Their first visit costs at least k+1 and each further distinct visit requires another write. If each is Mₖ−c for a positive seed or tower-spine value c available by time k, the joint optimum is exactly k+m: construct the maximum once and subtract each offset. For a capped machine the spine must fit. If the initial bank fits and there are at least m+1 registers, keep offsets as they arise, overwrite unneeded values while squaring, then use the dying offset's slot for each subtraction. For example 240, 252 and 255 from 1 have joint optimum seven. Four writes reach 256 and three subtractions use 16, 4 and 1. Every individual target exceeds the four-write runner-up 81. More generally, from seed 1 with k≥3 every proper earlier spine value gives a subtraction in the gap, since Mₖ₋₁≤Mₖ/4 while Sₖ/Mₖ≤9/16.

**What changes when subtraction is available.** For seed 1 let L be shortest positive four-operation length and d(n)=L(n)−1−log₂log₂n. For n=Mₖ−1, k≥3, the result above gives L=k+1. Its defect is strictly greater than 1 and strictly decreases to 1 with k. Indeed log(M−1)/log M increases strictly to 1, as is seen from 1+log(1−1/M)/log M. The signed real-defect set therefore fails to be well-ordered already below 2. In contrast the slice below 1 coincides exactly with the monotone addition–multiplication slice. A length-k witness with defect below 1 exceeds Mₖ₋₁ for k≥2. A step failing to increase the running peak would leave at most k−1 record steps, each increasing it by at most the usual tower growth, so all steps must be records. Only addition and multiplication can raise a positive peak. Conversely an AM witness below 1 cannot be shortened in the larger model, by the same maximum bound. The single first step is immediate. This result concerns unbounded arithmetic. Under any fixed finite magnitude cap only finitely many members of this sequence fit.
